I DREAM BIG BUT DO NOTHING. the neuroscience behind why & how to fix

Olga Loiek

Exports

Summary

Procrastination isn't about bad time management; it's about avoiding tough feelings a task might bring. Our brain tries to protect us from discomfort, creating a cycle of delay. The best way to beat it is to simply start with a tiny step.

LinkedIn Posts

What if I told you procrastination isn't a flaw in your work ethic, but a deeply wired emotional response? It's not about being lazy; it's your brain trying to shield you from tough feelings like overwhelm, self-doubt, or the fear of failure. Think of it: when you face a challenging task, your brain's alarm system goes off. You avoid the task, maybe scroll social media or clean. That temporary relief? It's a sneaky reward, strengthening the habit of procrastination every single time. You're building a neural pathway for avoidance. Here's the truth: you're not avoiding the task itself. You're avoiding how you *think* the task will make you feel. And guess what? Your brain is often totally wrong about that predicted dread. So, how do you break free? Just start. Pick the absolute tiniest step you can take for 5-10 minutes. Don't aim to finish it or even do it well. Simply begin. Open the document. Put on your running shoes. Make that first call. Naming the specific emotion you're feeling (e.g., "I'm feeling overwhelmed by this project") can also help. It calms your emotional brain and lets your rational brain step in. You'll find the actual doing is almost always much easier than the fear of starting. — _Created with CloudClick_
